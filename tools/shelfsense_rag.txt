========================================================
SECTION 2: PAR LEVELS & REORDER THRESHOLDS
========================================================
PAR (Periodic Automatic Replacement) level = minimum stock needed to last until next delivery.
Always keep stock ABOVE par level. If stock falls below par, flag as CRITICAL.

HOW TO CALCULATE PAR LEVEL:
  PAR = (Daily Usage × Lead Time in Days) + Safety Buffer (20% of weekly usage)

TYPICAL LEAD TIMES BY SUPPLIER TYPE:
- Broadline distributor (Sysco, US Foods):  2-3 days
- Local produce vendor:                     1-2 days
- Meat supplier:                            1-2 days
- Dairy supplier:                           1-2 days
- Dry goods / pantry:                       3-5 days

SAFETY BUFFER GUIDELINES:
- Perishables (meat, dairy, fresh produce):  add 15-20% buffer to order quantity
- Dry goods (pasta, canned goods):           add 10% buffer
- High-variability items (weekend specials): add 25% buffer

PRIORITY FLAGS:
- CRITICAL:  current stock < 20% of forecasted weekly need → order immediately
- HIGH:      current stock < 50% of forecasted weekly need → order this cycle
- NORMAL:    current stock between 50-100% of need → order standard quantity
- SKIP:      current stock > 150% of weekly need → do not order, risk of spoilage

SECTION 3: DEMAND MULTIPLIERS BY EVENT & CONDITION
========================================================
Apply these multipliers to baseline weekly demand when relevant conditions exist.
Multipliers are cumulative — a holiday on a rainy day = holiday multiplier × weather multiplier.

SPORTS EVENTS (home games)
- NFL home game (Sunday):           +30 to +50% traffic spike, especially wings, burgers, beer
- NBA / NHL home game (weeknight):  +20 to +35% spike during game hours
- College football home game:       +25 to +45% spike, especially near campus areas
- Championship / playoff game:      +50 to +80% spike — treat as major event
- Away games (sports bar):          +15 to +25% for sports bars specifically

CONCERTS & FESTIVALS
- Major concert at nearby venue:    +20 to +40% pre/post show (2hr window)
- Food festival in area:            -10 to -20% (people eating at festival instead)
- Street festival / block party:    +10 to +20% if restaurant is on route
- Convention / trade show:          +15 to +30% for lunch rush, corporate crowd

HOLIDAYS & SEASONAL
- Valentine's Day:                  +40 to +60% for dinner service, less for lunch
- Mother's Day:                     +50 to +70% — highest restaurant day of the year
- Father's Day:                     +30 to +45%
- New Year's Eve:                   +60 to +80% for dinner, reservations-heavy
- Thanksgiving week (Mon-Wed):      -20 to -30% as families cook at home
- Christmas Eve / Christmas Day:    -40 to -60% unless holiday-specific menu
- Fourth of July:                   -15 to -25% for lunch, +20% for dinner/bar
- Labor Day weekend:                +15 to +25%
- Spring Break (local schools):     +20 to +35% for family-friendly restaurants

WEATHER CONDITIONS
- Heavy rain / thunderstorm:        -15 to -25% dine-in, +10 to +20% delivery orders
- Light rain / overcast:            -5 to -10% dine-in
- Extreme heat (>95°F):             -10 to -15% lunch, -5% dinner
- Cold snap / snowstorm:            -25 to -40% dine-in
- Perfect weather (70-80°F sunny):  +10 to +15%, especially for patios
- First warm day of spring:         +20 to +30% patio effect

OTHER FACTORS
- School in session (weekday lunch): Slight boost for nearby restaurants
- School holiday / spring break:     +15 to +25% for family restaurants, varies
- Local road closure near restaurant: -20 to -35% depending on foot traffic dependency
- New competitor opened nearby:      -10 to -20% adjustment (monitor over 4 weeks)
- Local business / office closed:    -10 to -20% for lunch if office-dependent

SECTION 4: FOOD WASTE BENCHMARKS
========================================================
Industry averages for food waste. Use to flag over-ordering risks.

ACCEPTABLE WASTE RATES BY CATEGORY:
- Fresh produce (lettuce, tomatoes, herbs):   8 to 15% waste rate
- Proteins (meat, poultry, seafood):          3 to 8% waste rate
- Dairy (milk, cream, cheese):                5 to 10% waste rate
- Dry goods (pasta, rice, flour):             1 to 3% waste rate
- Prepared/cooked items held overnight:       15 to 25% waste rate

SHELF LIFE REFERENCE:
- Ground beef (refrigerated):     1-2 days raw, 3-4 days cooked
- Chicken breast (refrigerated):  1-2 days raw, 3-4 days cooked
- Leafy greens (romaine, lettuce): 5-7 days refrigerated
- Tomatoes:                       5-7 days at room temp, 2 weeks refrigerated
- Milk:                           5-7 days after opening
- Hard cheese (parmesan):         3-4 weeks refrigerated after opening
- Dry pasta:                      1-2 years sealed, use within 1 year opened
- Potatoes:                       3-5 weeks in cool dark storage

WASTE WARNING TRIGGERS:
- Order quantity > 130% of forecasted need for perishables → flag as waste risk
- Item has been on "skip" list for 2+ consecutive weeks → consider removing from menu
- Inventory of perishable item > 3 days worth of projected daily usage → order less

========================================================
SECTION 5: RESTAURANT INDUSTRY TERMINOLOGY
========================================================
Key terms ShelfSense uses when communicating with restaurant owners and managers.

86'd / Getting 86'd:    Running out of a menu item mid-service. "We got 86'd on salmon."
                        ShelfSense goal: prevent all 86'd situations.

PAR level:              Periodic Automatic Replacement — the minimum stock level that
                        triggers a reorder. Running below PAR = reorder immediately.

Mise en place:          French for "everything in its place." Refers to having all
                        ingredients prepped and ready before service begins.

Prep list:              Daily list of items that need to be prepped before service
                        (chopped, marinated, portioned, etc.)

Cover:                  One customer served = one cover. "We did 200 covers on Friday."
                        Used to estimate ingredient needs per service period.

Turn / Table turn:      How many times a table is seated in one service period.
                        Higher turns = more covers = more ingredient usage.

Food cost percentage:   (Cost of ingredients sold / Revenue) × 100.
                        Industry target: 28-35% for most restaurants.

Yield:                  Usable portion after trimming/cooking. Example: 1 lb of chicken
                        breast yields about 0.85 lbs after trimming. Account for yield
                        loss when calculating order quantities for proteins.

Daypart:                A segment of the day (breakfast, lunch, dinner, late night).
                        Demand forecasting should account for which dayparts are busiest.

FIFO:                   First In, First Out — older stock should always be used before
                        newer stock to reduce spoilage. Critical for perishables.

Comps / Voids:          Free items given to customers (comps) or cancelled orders (voids).
                        Typically adds 1-3% to ingredient usage beyond menu sales data.

Plate waste:            Food left uneaten on plates. High plate waste on a menu item
                        suggests portion sizes may be too large.

SECTION 6: ORDERING BEST PRACTICES
========================================================

ORDERING FREQUENCY GUIDELINES:
- Proteins (meat, seafood):         Order 3× per week or more if high volume
- Fresh produce:                    Order 2-3× per week
- Dairy:                            Order 2× per week
- Dry goods & non-perishables:      Order 1× per week
- Paper goods & supplies:           Order 1-2× per month

COST-SAVING TIPS TO MENTION:
- Buying proteins in bulk (case quantities) typically saves 10-20% vs. individual units
- Seasonal produce is 20-40% cheaper and higher quality than out-of-season
- Consolidating orders to fewer supplier deliveries reduces per-delivery fees
- Comparing 2-3 supplier quotes monthly can reduce food costs by 5-10%

COMMON ORDERING MISTAKES TO FLAG:
- Ordering perishables for more than 3-4 days of projected use
- Not adjusting orders for known slow periods (holidays, weather events)
- Ordering by habit (same quantity every week) instead of based on forecast
- Not accounting for upcoming menu specials that will spike specific ingredient demand
- Forgetting to deduct current on-hand stock before placing an order
